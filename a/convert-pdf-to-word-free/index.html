How to convert PDF to Word free in Microsoft Word (the no-tool method)

If you already have Microsoft Word, you don't need any converter at all. Word can open a PDF directly and turn it into an editable document — no upload, fully offline.

  1. Open Word.
  2. Click File > Open and browse to your PDF.
  3. Word shows a message saying it will make an editable copy. Click OK.
  4. Word converts the PDF into a new document. Your original PDF stays untouched — Word works on a copy.
  5. Edit as normal, then File > Save As and choose Word Document (.docx).

The honest caveat (straight from Microsoft): the converted file "might not have a perfect page-to-page correspondence with the original" — lines and pages can break in different spots. Microsoft says this method "works best with PDFs that are mostly text," and that book chapters or copier-scanned pages "won't be formatted well." (Source: Microsoft Support.)

So this is your best free choice for simple, text-heavy PDFs. For complex layouts, expect a quick cleanup pass.

Convert PDF to Word online free with Adobe (best for formatting)

Adobe invented the PDF format, so its converter tends to be one of the best at keeping fonts, images, and alignment in place. Adobe offers a free online PDF-to-Word tool with no subscription needed for a basic conversion.

  1. Go to Adobe's online "PDF to Word" tool in your browser.
  2. Drag and drop your PDF onto the page (or click to select it).
  3. Wait a few seconds while it converts.
  4. Download the editable Word file.

A few things to know: free online tools usually have daily usage limits, and you may or may not be asked to sign in — both can change, so check the tool before you count on it for a big batch. And because this method uploads your file to Adobe's servers, skip it for confidential documents (more on that below).

How to convert a scanned PDF to Word (free OCR with Google Docs)

Here's the part most guides skip. If your PDF is a scan or a photo of a page, it's really just an image — the "text" is a picture. Plain Word or LibreOffice can't edit picture-text. You first need OCR (optical character recognition), which reads the image and turns it back into real, editable letters.

The good news: Google Docs does OCR for free.

  1. Go to Google Drive and upload your scanned PDF.
  2. Right-click the file > Open with > Google Docs.
  3. Google runs OCR and opens a new Doc with the text extracted above an image of each page.
  4. Clean up the text, then choose File > Download > Microsoft Word (.docx).

Google's official limits (worth knowing so you're not surprised): the file should be 2 MB or smaller, right-side-up, and sharp; use common fonts like Arial or Times New Roman for best results. Bold, italics, font size and line breaks are usually kept, but lists, tables, columns, and footnotes are often not detected, and OCR pulls text from roughly the first pages only. (Source: Google Drive Help.) Google adjusts these caps over time, so verify the current ones if your file is large.

Free offline option — LibreOffice (no upload)

Want a free converter that never sends your file anywhere? LibreOffice is free, open-source, and works entirely on your computer — ideal when privacy matters or you're offline.

  1. Install LibreOffice (free).
  2. Open your PDF in LibreOffice. By default it opens in LibreOffice Draw.
  3. Edit if needed, then go to File > Export (or Save As) and choose a Word-compatible format (.docx).

Where it struggles (be realistic): LibreOffice handles simple, text-based PDFs well, but multi-column layouts, tables, and graphics-heavy pages can break apart and need manual fixing. And like plain Word, it has no built-in OCR for this, so scanned PDFs won't work unless you OCR them first (use the Google Docs route above for those). (Source: LibreOffice community guides.)

Are free PDF-to-Word converters safe?

Mostly yes, for ordinary files — but with one important rule.

Reputable online services protect your upload with TLS/SSL encryption in transit and auto-delete files after a set window. For example, Smallpdf says it's GDPR-compliant and ISO/IEC 27001 certified, with files auto-deleted after about an hour; iLovePDF is ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certified, GDPR-compliant, and says uploads aren't used to train models. (Sources: Smallpdf and iLovePDF.)

But here's the rule that matters: for sensitive or confidential documents — contracts, medical or legal paperwork, anything with personal data — the real risk isn't a specific brand, it's the upload itself. The safest move is a method that never uploads your file at all:

  • Open it directly in Microsoft Word (offline), or
  • Convert it in LibreOffice (offline).

Save the online converters for non-sensitive files, and you get the convenience without the exposure.

Tips to convert PDF to Word without losing formatting

A few habits keep your converted file clean:

  • The more complex the PDF, the more cleanup. Lots of tables, columns, or images means any tool will introduce small errors — budget a few minutes to fix them in Word.
  • Always enable OCR for scans. If your PDF is a scan and you skip OCR (or "Advanced mode"), you'll get a non-editable image, not text.
  • Check tables and columns first after converting — they're the most likely things to shift or merge.
  • Keep a copy of the original PDF. Convert on a copy so you can always go back if the layout breaks badly.
  • Use common fonts for scanned files — OCR reads Arial or Times New Roman far more reliably than decorative fonts.

FAQ

What's the best free way to convert a PDF to Word without losing formatting?
For a normal text PDF, open it directly in Microsoft Word (File > Open) — it keeps text well and stays offline. For the closest layout match on a complex file, Adobe's free online tool is a strong pick. Expect minor cleanup either way.

How do I convert a scanned PDF to Word?
A scanned PDF is an image, so it needs OCR first. Upload it to Google Drive, right-click > Open with > Google Docs (this runs OCR), then File > Download > Microsoft Word (.docx).

Can I convert a PDF to Word without signing up?
Often, yes. Opening a PDF in Word or LibreOffice needs no account. Adobe and several browser tools frequently allow conversions without sign-up, though some prompt you to sign in or cap free use — check the tool when you go.

Is it safe to upload my PDF to a free converter?
Reputable tools encrypt uploads and auto-delete them, which is fine for ordinary files. But for confidential documents, don't upload at all — use the offline Word or LibreOffice route instead.

Why is my converted Word file messed up (tables and columns)?
Tables, multi-column layouts, and graphics are the hardest things to convert cleanly. Every tool struggles with them, so a quick manual cleanup in Word is normal — pick the tool with the best formatting fidelity and plan a fix-up pass.

Can I convert a PDF to Word on my phone?
Yes — the browser-based tools (Adobe, Smallpdf, iLovePDF, and others) work in a mobile browser, and Google Drive's "Open with Google Docs" OCR works on mobile too. The same upload caution applies for sensitive files.
