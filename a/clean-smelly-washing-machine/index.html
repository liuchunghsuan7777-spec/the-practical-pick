How to Clean a Smelly Washing Machine (Front & Top Load)

Quick answer: To clean a smelly washing machine, wipe down the rubber gasket and detergent dispensers, then run an empty hot cycle with either white vinegar or chlorine bleach — never both — and leave the door open to air-dry. Repeat about once a month, and you'll keep that musty, mildew odor from coming back.

If your laundry comes out smelling worse than when it went in, the washer itself is almost always the culprit. The good news: you can usually fix it today with things you already have at home. Below you'll find separate step-by-step methods for front-load and top-load machines, a how-often cleaning table, and one important exception — when the smell points to your plumbing, not your washer.

Why does my washing machine smell?

A washer is warm, damp, and full of leftover residue — basically a spa for mold and bacteria. Here's what's usually behind the odor:

  • Mildew and mold buildup. Cool-only wash cycles, leaving wet laundry sitting in the drum, and shutting the door right after unloading all trap moisture and let mold grow. This is the classic musty, damp-towel smell.
  • Detergent and fabric-softener residue. Combined with body oils, lint, and hair, this gunk collects in the door seal, dispensers, and crevices and feeds odor-causing bacteria — the sour or stale smell.
  • Front-loaders are extra prone to gasket mold. That tight rubber door seal is great for stopping leaks, but it also traps water and soap in its folds, which is exactly where mold loves to live.

When the smell is your plumbing, not your washer

Here's the differentiator most articles skip: a sewage or rotten-egg smell is often not the washer at all. That points to a drain or plumbing issue — a clogged or dry standpipe, sewer gases backing up, or a venting problem. If you've already deep-cleaned the drum and the rotten-egg smell remains, stop scrubbing and check the drain. Pouring a quart of water down the standpipe to refill a dried-out trap is a common first fix; if it persists, call a plumber. Cleaning the drum won't solve a plumbing problem.

Good to Know
Drum Smell vs. Drain Smell
Not all washer odors come from the machine. A musty, damp-towel smell is usually mold and residue in the drum, gasket, or dispensers, which cleaning fixes. But a sewage or rotten-egg smell more often points to a drain or plumbing issue, such as a dried-out standpipe trap, a clog, or sewer gases venting back. Try refilling the trap by pouring a quart of water down the standpipe; if the smell stays after a full drum clean, it is a plumbing job, not a scrubbing one. What you'll need

A short checklist before you start. You only need one of the two cleaning agents (vinegar OR bleach) per session — not both.

  • White vinegar or chlorine bleach (pick one — see the safety note)
  • Baking soda (for top-load method)
  • Microfiber cloths
  • An old toothbrush or small detail brush
  • All-purpose cleaner for the gasket and exterior
  • Rubber gloves
Info
Washer Cleaner: Tablets vs. Vinegar or Bleach
You have a few options for the actual cleaning agent. White vinegar is a budget, low-residue choice that handles most musty odors and is gentle in moderation, but used too often it can wear rubber seals. Chlorine bleach is stronger on heavy mold and sanitizing, but must be used as directed and never mixed with other cleaners. Dedicated washer-cleaner tablets are the mess-free, pre-measured option and pair well with a machine's tub-clean cycle, trading a little cost for convenience. Match your choice to how strong the odor is and what your manual allows. Safety first: never mix bleach and vinegar

This matters, so it's worth saying plainly:

  • Never combine bleach with vinegar (or with any other cleaner). The mix releases toxic chlorine gas. Choose one method per cleaning and rinse the drum between if you ever switch.
  • Check your appliance's care guide before using bleach. Bleach is generally safe in standard and HE washers when used as directed, but confirm with your manual first.
  • Go easy on vinegar — about once a month at most. Frequent acidic cycles can wear down rubber seals and hoses over time.

How to clean a front-load washer (gasket + drum)

Front-loaders need the door seal cleaned first — that rubber gasket is where most of the mold hides.

  1. Wipe the rubber gasket. Gently pull back the folds of the door seal and wipe inside with a microfiber cloth and all-purpose cleaner. Get into every crease — this is where black mildew and trapped residue collect.
  2. Clean the dispensers. Slide out the detergent and softener drawers (most pop out fully) and wash them in warm, soapy water. Use the toothbrush for clogged corners, then dry and replace.
  3. Run an empty hot cycle with a cleaning agent. Choose one:
    - Vinegar: add about 1 gallon of white vinegar to the empty drum and run the hottest, longest cycle.
    - Bleach: add about 2 cups of chlorine bleach. If your washer allows, pause the cycle when the drum is full, let it soak ~30 minutes, then resume.
    - Many machines also have a dedicated "clean washer" / "tub clean" cycle — if yours does, use a washer-cleaner tablet with it.
  4. Wipe and air-dry. Wipe the drum and glass, then leave the door open so the inside dries fully. Trapped moisture is what restarts the smell.

How to clean a top-load washer with vinegar & baking soda

For a top-loader, the classic two-cycle vinegar-then-baking-soda combo works well and is gentle on the machine.

  1. Hot cycle + vinegar soak. Start the hottest cycle and let the drum fill for about a minute. Add roughly 1 quart of white vinegar, let it agitate briefly to mix, then pause and let it soak for about an hour. Resume and finish the cycle.
  2. Second hot cycle + baking soda. Run another hot cycle, this time adding about 1 cup of baking soda directly to the drum. This neutralizes lingering odors and helps rinse loosened residue.
  3. Wipe and detail. With a microfiber cloth, wipe the top inside rim of the tub (mildew loves that lip). If your agitator cap lifts off, clean underneath it, and scrub the dispensers with the toothbrush. Leave the lid open to dry.

How often should you clean a washing machine?

Most manufacturers recommend a deep clean about once a month, or roughly every 30 wash cycles. Different parts need different attention:

Part How often What to do
Drum / tub Monthly (~every 30 cycles) Empty hot cycle with vinegar or bleach, or a tub-clean cycle
Door gasket / seal (front-load) Weekly wipe + monthly deep clean Wipe folds dry; clean with all-purpose cleaner
Detergent dispensers Monthly Remove and wash in warm soapy water
Drain pump filter (front-load) Every 3–4 months Check your manual; drain and clear trapped lint/debris

Run the deep-clean cycle more often if you wash heavily soiled loads, use cold water exclusively, or live somewhere humid.

Prevent the smell from coming back

Cleaning fixes today's odor — these habits stop it from returning:

  • Wipe the gasket and door dry after a wash, especially on front-loaders.
  • Leave the door or lid open between loads so air can circulate and the drum dries out.
  • Remove wet laundry promptly — don't let a finished load sit overnight.
  • Use the right amount of detergent. Overdosing leaves residue; HE machines need HE detergent in smaller amounts.
  • Run a monthly clean cycle as routine maintenance, not just when it already smells.

FAQ

Why does my washer smell like sewage or rotten eggs?
That specific smell usually points to your drain or plumbing, not the washer drum — a dried-out or clogged standpipe trap, or sewer gases venting back. Try refilling the trap with water; if it lingers after a full drum clean, call a plumber.

Can I use vinegar and bleach together?
No. Mixing them creates toxic fumes. Pick one method per cleaning session, and never combine bleach with any other cleaner.

How much vinegar do I need to clean a washing machine?
Roughly 1 gallon for a front-load empty hot cycle, or about 1 quart added to a filling top-load drum for a soak. Keep vinegar cleans to about once a month to protect the rubber seals.

Will cleaning the washer remove the mildew smell from my clothes?
It removes the source, so future loads come out fresh. Clothes that already smell musty may need a separate rewash — try a hot cycle with vinegar (no detergent) on those items.

Are washer-cleaner tablets worth it?
They're a convenient, mess-free option, especially with a machine's dedicated tub-clean cycle. Vinegar or bleach work just as well for most odors; tablets simply trade a little cost for convenience.

Bottom line

A smelly washing machine almost always comes down to trapped moisture and residue, and the fix is straightforward: wipe the gasket and dispensers, run a hot cycle with vinegar or bleach (never both), and air-dry with the door open. Make it a monthly habit, and don't forget the one exception — a rotten-egg, sewage smell usually means it's time to look at the drain, not the drum.
