How to Clean a Mattress: Remove Stains, Odor & Dust Mites

Quick answer: To clean a mattress, strip and hot-wash the bedding, vacuum the whole mattress with an upholstery tool (including the sides and seams), spot-clean any stains, sprinkle a thin layer of baking soda and let it sit for several hours, then vacuum it off. Air it out until fully dry and add a washable, waterproof protector. Deep-clean a mattress about every six months, and treat spills right away.

You spend a third of your life on your mattress, but it almost never gets cleaned — so it quietly collects sweat, body oils, dead skin, dust mites, and the occasional accident. The good news: you can deep-clean it today with things you already have, no special machine required. Below you'll find the full step-by-step routine, a stain-by-stain table (urine, sweat, blood), a dust-mite plan, a how-often schedule, and the one rule that saves a memory-foam mattress from mold.

What you'll need

You don't need anything fancy. Most of this is already in your kitchen or laundry cupboard.

  • A vacuum with an upholstery attachment (a HEPA filter helps trap dust-mite allergens)
  • Baking soda (about 1 cup for a full mattress)
  • White vinegar
  • Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
  • A little dish soap
  • An enzyme cleaner (optional but excellent for urine and pet accidents)
  • A few clean white cloths or microfiber towels
  • A washable, waterproof mattress protector (for afterward)

Compare — Which Cleaner for Which Stain?

Each cleaner has a job. Baking soda is your all-purpose deodorizer and moisture absorber — great for everyday freshening and the final step of any clean. A 50/50 white vinegar and water spray neutralizes fresh urine odor and lifts light marks. Hydrogen peroxide (often mixed with a little baking soda and dish soap) is the heavy hitter for set-in urine and yellowing, but it can lighten colored fabric, so spot-test first. An enzyme cleaner is the specialist for protein-based messes — urine, sweat, and pet accidents — because it breaks the odor down at the source instead of masking it. Pick by the mess in front of you, and never mix hydrogen peroxide or bleach with vinegar. A few safety rules first

Worth saying plainly before you start:

  • Apply cleaner to the cloth, not directly onto the mattress. Blot — don't rub. Rubbing pushes liquid (and the stain) deeper into the foam.
  • Use cold water on blood stains. Heat sets protein-based stains and makes them permanent.
  • Never mix hydrogen peroxide or bleach with vinegar. The combinations are useless at best and can release irritating or toxic fumes.
  • Go easy on moisture, especially with memory foam. A soaked mattress that can't dry grows mold. Less liquid, then full drying, always.

How to clean a mattress step by step

This is the core routine. It works for any mattress; the only difference for memory foam is using as little moisture as possible (more on that below).

  1. Strip the bed and hot-wash everything. Pull off the sheets, pillowcases, and mattress protector and wash them in the hottest water the labels allow — heat is what kills dust mites living in your bedding.
  2. Vacuum the whole mattress. Use the upholstery attachment and go over the entire top, then the sides and every seam and crease, where dust and dead skin collect. Take your time here.
  3. Spot-clean any stains. Treat urine, sweat, or blood marks now, using the stain-specific methods in the next section. Blot with a damp cloth, don't soak.
  4. Sprinkle baking soda over the surface. A thin, even layer across the whole mattress absorbs moisture and neutralizes odor. Let it sit several hours — overnight if you can.
  5. Vacuum the baking soda off. Go slowly so you pick up all of it.
  6. Flip or rotate and repeat if your mattress is double-sided, then air it out — open a window, or let it sit in sunlight if you can — until it's completely dry.
  7. Add a clean protector. Once dry, put on a washable, waterproof protector before remaking the bed. It's the single best way to keep this clean lasting.

How to remove specific stains

Different stains need different chemistry. Here's the quick version, then the details.

Stain Use Key rule
Fresh urine Blot, 50/50 vinegar-water spray, then baking soda Cover with baking soda for 8+ hours, then vacuum
Set-in urine / yellowing Hydrogen peroxide + baking soda + a drop of dish soap Spot-test first; let dry, then vacuum residue
Sweat / yellow stains Baking soda (dry or as a paste) Yellowing is usually body oils, not urine
Blood Cold water + mild detergent or hydrogen peroxide Cold water only — heat sets it

Urine

For a fresh accident, blot up as much liquid as you can with a dry towel. Lightly mist the area with a 50/50 white vinegar and water solution, let it soak in for about 15 minutes, then blot again. Cover the spot with a layer of baking soda and leave it 8 to 10 hours (overnight is ideal) before vacuuming. Repeat if any smell or mark lingers.

For a set-in or old stain, mix about 1 cup hydrogen peroxide, 3 tablespoons baking soda, and 2 drops of dish soap. Spot-test on a hidden corner first, then lightly dampen the stain — start small. Let it dry completely, then vacuum up the powdery residue. For pet and child accidents, or any odor that won't quit, an enzyme cleaner is worth it: it breaks down the proteins in urine rather than just covering the smell.

Sweat and yellow stains

Those yellowish patches are usually sweat and body oils, not necessarily urine. Sprinkle baking soda over the area and leave it 30 to 60 minutes to pull out moisture and odor, then vacuum. For tougher marks, make a baking soda and water paste, spread a thin layer on the stain, let it sit 1 to 2 hours until dry, and vacuum it off.

Blood

The one rule: cold water only. Warm or hot water cooks the protein in blood and locks the stain in for good. Blot (don't rub) with a cloth dampened in cold water mixed with a little mild detergent, or dab with hydrogen peroxide for stubborn spots. Work from the outside of the stain inward, and keep blotting with a clean part of the cloth.

Important — Cleaning a Memory Foam Mattress

Memory foam is not designed to get wet. Trapped moisture deep in the foam can lead to mold, so use as little cleaning solution as possible — mist or dab, never soak — and always let the mattress dry completely before you put bedding back on. Stick to dry methods where you can: vacuuming and baking soda do most of the work. When you do need to spot-treat a stain, apply the solution to a cloth, blot gently, and give the area plenty of airflow afterward. How to deodorize a musty mattress

If there's no specific stain but the mattress just smells stale, baking soda is the whole answer. Sprinkle a thin, even layer across the entire surface and leave it for several hours — the longer it sits, the more odor and moisture it absorbs. Then vacuum it all up. For a deeper refresh, do this on a dry, sunny day with a window open: airflow and sunlight both help drive out musty smells and moisture. Repeat the baking soda pass if the odor is strong.

How to get rid of dust mites in a mattress

Dust mites feed on dead skin and thrive in warm, humid bedding. You can't sterilize a mattress, but you can knock their numbers way down and keep them there. Three things do the heavy lifting:

  • Vacuum with a HEPA-filter vacuum. Go over the surface and seams to capture mites, eggs, and the allergen particles they leave behind. A HEPA filter keeps that fine dust from blowing back into the room.
  • Use heat. Wash all bedding in hot water (around 130°F / 54°C or hotter), which kills mites and their eggs. A handheld steam cleaner passed over the mattress surface adds heat where vacuuming can't reach — just keep moisture light and let it dry fully.
  • Seal it off with an allergen protector. A dust-mite-proof (allergen-barrier) mattress protector is the real long-term control. It blocks mites from settling into the mattress and is easy to wash on a hot cycle.

Keeping bedroom humidity down helps too, since mites struggle in dry air. Note this is general home-care guidance — reducing allergen exposure may help, but it isn't a treatment for any medical condition.

Compare — Mattress Protector: Waterproof vs. Allergen-Barrier

A protector is what keeps this clean from undoing itself, and there are two main types. A waterproof protector is the priority if spills, kids, or pets are your concern — it stops urine, sweat, and liquids from ever reaching the mattress, though cheaper plastic-backed versions can sleep warm. An allergen-barrier (dust-mite-proof) encasement uses a tight-weave fabric that mites and their droppings can't pass through, which is the better pick if allergies are the main issue; full-encasement styles zip around the whole mattress for the most coverage. Many protectors now do both — waterproof and allergen-blocking — and most are machine-washable on a hot cycle. Choose by whether spills or allergens are your bigger problem, and check the fit for your mattress depth. How often should you clean a mattress?

A little routine maintenance means you'll rarely need a heavy deep clean. Here's a simple schedule:

Task How often
Wash sheets and pillowcases Weekly, in hot water
Vacuum the mattress Monthly (more often for allergy sufferers)
Deep-clean (vacuum + spot-treat + baking soda) About every 6 months
Rotate or flip the mattress Per the maker's guidance (often every 3–6 months)
Treat spills and accidents Immediately

Address any spill the moment it happens — fresh stains lift far more easily than set-in ones, and you'll avoid odor altogether.

FAQ

How do I get pee out of a mattress?
For a fresh accident, blot it dry, mist with a 50/50 vinegar-and-water solution, wait 15 minutes, then cover with baking soda for 8 or more hours and vacuum. For old, set-in urine, use a mix of hydrogen peroxide, baking soda, and a drop of dish soap (spot-test first), or an enzyme cleaner to break down the odor at its source.

Why is my mattress turning yellow?
Usually sweat and body oils soaking in over time — not necessarily urine. Baking soda (dry, or as a paste) lifts most yellowing; for stubborn spots, a light hydrogen-peroxide mix helps, but spot-test first since it can lighten fabric.

Can you clean a memory foam mattress with water?
Use as little water as possible. Memory foam isn't meant to get wet, and trapped moisture can grow mold. Mist or dab — never soak — and let it dry completely before remaking the bed. Dry methods (vacuum + baking soda) should do most of the work.

How long does baking soda need to sit on a mattress?
At least several hours, and ideally overnight (8 or more hours). The longer it sits, the more odor and moisture it pulls out before you vacuum it off.

How often should you replace a mattress?
Most mattresses last around 7 to 10 years, depending on type and quality. Regular cleaning and a protector help it reach the high end of that range — but sagging, lumps, or waking up sore are signs it's time to shop.

Bottom line

Cleaning a mattress comes down to a simple rhythm: vacuum, spot-treat any stains, deodorize with baking soda, and dry it fully — then protect it. Match the cleaner to the stain (vinegar and baking soda for fresh urine, hydrogen peroxide for set-in marks, cold water for blood), keep moisture minimal on memory foam, and lean on a HEPA vacuum plus an allergen protector for dust mites. Do the full routine about every six months, wash your sheets weekly, and your bed stays fresh with very little effort.
