How to Find a Lost or Stolen Phone (Android & iPhone)

Step-by-step: locate a lost iPhone with Find My or an Android with Find My Device, play a sound, lock or erase it remotely, and prevent it next time.

Losing your phone is stressful, but you can often locate it in just a few minutes. Take a breath, grab any device with a web browser, and follow the steps below for your phone type.

Find a lost iPhone

Apple's Find My system works even if your iPhone is locked, as long as it's turned on and connected to a network. You can also locate a nearby iPhone that's offline through Apple's Bluetooth network.

  1. On another Apple device, open the Find My app and tap the Devices tab. Or, from any computer, go to iCloud.com/find and sign in with your Apple ID.
  2. Select your iPhone from the list. Its last known location appears on the map.
  3. If it's nearby, tap Play Sound to make it ring, even on silent.
  4. Tap Directions to open the location in Maps and navigate toward it.

If you can't sign in, use another trusted device or the recovery steps at Apple's account page. Make sure you're using the same Apple ID that's signed in on the lost phone.

Find a lost Android phone

Google's Find My Device (recently expanded as the Find My Device network) helps you locate most Android phones tied to your Google account.

  1. On a computer or another phone, go to google.com/android/find or open the Find My Device app.
  2. Sign in with the Google account that's on your lost phone. If you have more than one phone, pick the right one from the top of the screen.
  3. The map shows the phone's current or last known location.
  4. Tap Play Sound to ring it at full volume for a few minutes, even if it's set to silent.

For this to work, the phone generally needs to be turned on, signed in to your Google account, connected to data or Wi-Fi, and have location turned on.

What to do, and in what order

Work from the least drastic option to the most drastic. You can always escalate, but erasing a phone is hard to undo.

  1. Play a sound first. If you think the phone is close, ring it before anything else. Many "lost" phones are just under a couch cushion or in a coat pocket.
  2. Mark it as lost. On iPhone, use Mark As Lost (Lost Mode); on Android, use Secure Device. This locks the screen, shows a custom message with a callback number, and suspends mobile wallet cards.
  3. Lock it. If it wasn't already protected by a passcode, marking it as lost will require one. This keeps your data private while you keep searching.
  4. Erase it only as a last resort. If you're confident the phone is gone for good, use Erase iPhone or Erase Device to wipe your personal data remotely. On iPhone, keep in mind that after a full erase you may lose the ability to track it, so try locating it first.

If your phone was stolen

Your safety matters more than any device. Do not go to the location yourself to retrieve it.

  • Do not confront a thief. Even if the map shows an exact spot, never approach a stranger to demand your phone back. Let the authorities handle it.
  • File a police report. Provide the phone's make, model, and IMEI or serial number (you can find these in your carrier account or original box). A report number is often required for insurance claims.
  • Contact your carrier. Ask them to suspend service so no one can rack up charges, and to flag or blacklist the IMEI so the device can't be reactivated.
  • Change key passwords. Update your email, banking, and Apple or Google account passwords, and sign the device out of those accounts remotely.

Prevent it next time

A few small habits make a lost phone far less likely, and much easier to find if it happens again.

  • Attach a Bluetooth item tracker. Slipping a small tracker into your phone case, or keeping one on your keychain next to your phone, gives you a second way to ping its location from a nearby app.
  • Keep it tethered to you. A phone lanyard or a grip on the back makes it harder to drop or leave behind at a restaurant, gym, or rideshare.
  • Stay charged. A phone with a dead battery can't be tracked. Carrying a charged power bank keeps your phone online long enough to show up on the map.
  • Turn on tracking now. Confirm Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) is enabled before you ever need it, and set a strong passcode plus screen lock.

Bottom line

If your phone goes missing, stay calm and start with Find My iPhone or Find My Device. Ring it, mark it as lost, and only erase it if recovery looks hopeless. If it was stolen, skip the heroics: file a report, call your carrier, and lock down your accounts. A little prevention today, a tracker, a lanyard, a charged power bank, means a lot less panic tomorrow.
