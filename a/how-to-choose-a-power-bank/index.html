How to Choose a Power Bank (Capacity & Fast Charging)

mAh, fast charging, and ports explained — pick a portable charger that actually keeps your devices going.

A power bank keeps your phone, tablet, and other gadgets running when you're away from an outlet, but the specs can feel confusing at first glance. This guide breaks down the numbers that matter so you can match a portable charger to the way you actually use it.

What to look for

Capacity is the headline number, listed in milliamp-hours (mAh). It tells you how much energy the battery holds, but the rated figure isn't the same as what reaches your device. Voltage conversion and heat cost you some energy along the way, so real-world output is lower than the label. As a rough mental model, a 10,000mAh power bank will typically top off a modern smartphone somewhere around one and a half to two full charges, though the exact result depends on your phone's own battery size.

Output wattage matters just as much as capacity, because it decides how fast your device refills. Look at the wattage a port can deliver, not just the amperage. Higher-wattage output is what makes a fast-charging power bank feel genuinely quick, and it's especially noticeable when you're topping up during a short break rather than overnight.

USB-C Power Delivery (PD) is the standard to prioritize. PD lets the power bank and your device negotiate the right voltage and wattage automatically, which supports faster, more efficient charging across a wide range of phones, tablets, and even some laptops. A USB-C PD port also tends to be the most future-proof, since newer devices increasingly rely on it.

Think about the number and type of ports too. A single USB-C port is fine if you only charge one thing at a time, but two or more ports let you power a phone and earbuds together. Keep in mind that total output is usually shared, so charging several devices at once can slow each one down.

Size and weight follow directly from capacity. Bigger batteries hold more energy but weigh more and take up more pocket or bag space, so there's always a trade-off between endurance and portability. Consider how you'll carry it day to day.

Pass-through charging, where the power bank charges your device while it's itself plugged into a wall, is a handy convenience if you want to use one outlet for everything overnight. Not every model supports it well, so check the description if that feature matters to you.

Finally, mind the airline rules. In the United States, lithium battery power banks must go in your carry-on, never checked luggage, and airlines commonly cap what you can bring around the 100 watt-hour mark without special approval. You can estimate watt-hours by multiplying capacity in amp-hours by the battery voltage, and most everyday power banks fall comfortably under the limit. Always confirm the current policy with your airline before you fly.

Matching capacity to how you'll use it

The right size depends on your routine rather than on picking the biggest number available.

For daily carry, a 10,000mAh power bank hits a sweet spot for many people. It's slim enough to slip into a jacket pocket or small bag, yet it holds enough charge to rescue a phone through a long day of navigation, photos, and messaging.

If you travel, work long shifts, or want to charge more than one device, step up to something larger. A high-capacity power bank in the 20,000mAh range and above can refill a phone several times or give a tablet a meaningful boost, which is useful for weekend trips, camping, or days when you simply can't count on finding an outlet.

For laptops and other power-hungry gear, focus on both capacity and wattage. A high-capacity power bank paired with a robust USB-C PD port is what lets you charge a laptop away from a desk, so check that the port's wattage is high enough for your machine.

If you mostly need a small safety net for earbuds or a smartwatch, a compact battery is plenty, and you'll appreciate carrying less weight.

Practical buying tips

Match the power bank's own recharge speed to your patience: larger batteries take longer to refill, so a model that itself accepts fast USB-C input will be back in service sooner. Check that the included cable supports the wattage you're paying for, since an underrated cable can quietly bottleneck charging. And prioritize a design that fits your bag and hand comfortably, because the best power bank is the one you'll actually bring along.

Bottom line

Start with how much backup power you realistically need, then confirm the output supports fast charging over USB-C PD. A 10,000mAh power bank covers most daily needs, a USB-C fast-charging power bank speeds up every top-up, and a high-capacity power bank is worth the extra weight when you're away from outlets for longer stretches. Match those factors to your routine and you'll pick a charger that keeps you powered without carrying more than you need.
