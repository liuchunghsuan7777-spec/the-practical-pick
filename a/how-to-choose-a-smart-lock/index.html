How to Choose a Smart Lock (Keypad, Wi-Fi & Compatibility)

Keypad vs app, Wi-Fi, and door compatibility — pick a smart lock that fits your door and routine.

Upgrading your front door with a smart lock can mean never fumbling for keys again, but the options move fast and the specs can be confusing. This guide walks through the features that actually matter so you can pick a lock that fits your door, your phone, and your daily routine.

What to look for

The first decision is how you want to get in. Most models offer several unlock methods, and the best choice usually mixes a few. A keypad door lock lets anyone in your household use a numeric code, which is handy for kids who lose keys or a guest who needs one-time access. App-based unlocking uses your smartphone over Bluetooth or your home network, so the door can open as you approach. Fingerprint readers add a fast, contactless option that doesn't require remembering a code. Look for a model that supports at least two of these, since a backup method saves you on the day your phone battery dies.

Connectivity is the next big fork. A Bluetooth-only lock talks directly to your phone within about a room's distance, which keeps things simple and easy on the batteries but limits remote features. A Wi-Fi smart lock connects to your home router, so you can lock or unlock the door, check its status, and grant access codes from anywhere with a signal. Some designs use Wi-Fi built into the lock; others rely on a separate bridge or hub that plugs in nearby. If you travel, run a rental, or simply like checking that the door is locked from the office, Wi-Fi is worth the trade-off in power use.

Security features deserve a close read. Consider how access codes are managed, whether temporary or scheduled codes are supported, and whether the lock keeps an activity log so you can see who came and went. Auto-lock, which secures the door after a set delay, closes the gap left by a forgotten manual lock. Tamper alerts and encrypted communication between the lock and your phone are also worth confirming in the product details.

Door and deadbolt compatibility

A smart lock only helps if it fits your door. Most models replace a standard deadbolt, so measure your door's thickness, the backset (the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the bore), and the diameter of the existing hole. Note whether your door is left- or right-handed and whether it's a solid slab or has a narrow stile, since some designs need more room than others. Certain smart locks keep your existing exterior hardware and only swap the interior thumbturn, which is a good route if you want to keep your current keys working alongside the new features.

Backup keys and batteries

Because these locks run on power, plan for the moments when power runs low. Check how the lock is powered, typically replaceable AA batteries or a rechargeable pack, and look for low-battery warnings sent to your phone or shown on the keypad. Many models keep a traditional keyway as a fallback, so a physical key still opens the door if the electronics fail. Others provide an emergency power contact where you can briefly touch a battery to wake a dead lock long enough to enter your code. Decide which backup approach gives you peace of mind before you buy.

Smart-home integration

If you already use a voice assistant or a broader smart-home setup, make sure the lock speaks the same language. Compatibility varies, so confirm the lock works with the ecosystem you use before assuming it will. Good integration lets you include the door in routines, such as locking up when you say goodnight, and check status alongside your other devices. If you don't use a smart-home platform today, a self-contained app is perfectly fine, and you can always expand later.

Installation and compatibility check

Before ordering, do a quick self-audit. Confirm your door type and measurements match the model's requirements, verify your Wi-Fi reaches the door if you want remote access, and read the setup steps to gauge whether it's a screwdriver-only job or something more involved. Most deadbolt replacements are a manageable weekend task, but a poorly fitting lock is a headache no feature list can fix. Testing the door's alignment first, so the bolt slides freely, prevents most installation frustration.

Bottom line

Choose based on how you actually enter your home and how much remote control you want. A keypad handles shared access, Bluetooth keeps things simple, and a Wi-Fi model unlocks true anywhere control. Match the lock to your door's measurements, confirm a reliable backup for dead batteries, and check smart-home compatibility before you buy. Nail those basics and a smart lock becomes one of the most convenient upgrades your front door will ever get.
