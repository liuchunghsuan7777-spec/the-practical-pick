How to Choose a Countertop Ice Maker (Nugget & Portable)

Ice type, speed, and capacity — pick a countertop ice maker with no plumbing required.

A countertop ice maker is one of the easiest upgrades you can make to a kitchen, home bar, or office break room, delivering a steady supply of ice without a plumbing hookup. This guide walks through the choices that actually matter so you can match a machine to how you drink, host, and store.

Ice type: nugget vs. bullet

The single biggest decision is the shape of ice a machine produces. A nugget ice maker creates soft, chewable pellets, sometimes called pebble or "the good ice," that soak up the flavor of whatever they sit in and are gentle on teeth. Nugget ice tends to cool drinks quickly and is a favorite for sodas, cocktails, and iced coffee.

Bullet ice, the cylindrical pieces most portable models produce, is faster to make and works well for everyday water, tea, and mixed drinks. Bullet ice is hollow and clears out of the machine quickly, but it also melts a bit faster than a dense nugget. If chewable texture is the priority, lean nugget; if speed and simplicity matter most, bullet is a practical pick.

Production speed and daily capacity

Two numbers describe output: how fast the first batch is ready, and how much ice a machine can make across a full day. A portable ice maker generally produces its first batch in a matter of minutes, which is handy when guests arrive unexpectedly. Nugget machines usually take longer to get going because the ice is formed and compacted differently.

Think about your peak demand rather than the average. A household that fills a few glasses a day has very different needs than someone hosting weekend gatherings or keeping a cooler stocked. Higher daily output costs more and often takes up more space, so it is worth being honest about how much ice you actually go through.

Storage bin and melt

Most countertop units are makers, not freezers, so the storage bin holds finished ice but does not keep it frozen indefinitely. Unused ice slowly melts and the water is typically recycled into the next batch, which is efficient but means the bin is not long-term storage. Check the bin capacity and how easy it is to reach with the included scoop. If you need ice to stay solid for hours, plan to move it to a freezer or an insulated cooler.

Self-cleaning, size, and portability

Because these machines constantly cycle water, regular cleaning keeps them running well and the ice tasting fresh. Many models include a self-cleaning or clean cycle that flushes the system with a cleaning solution, which saves time and reduces guesswork. Even with that feature, plan on occasional manual wipe-downs of the bin and reservoir.

Size and portability go hand in hand. Measure your counter depth and the clearance under cabinets before buying, since taller units need room for the lid to open. A lighter machine is easier to move to a patio, RV, or party table, while a larger one is better left in one spot. Look for a clear window or indicator lights so you can see ice levels and low-water warnings at a glance.

No plumbing needed

A major appeal of these units is that they require no water line. You pour water into a reservoir, plug the machine into a standard outlet, and it does the rest. That makes a countertop ice maker a renter-friendly, apartment-friendly option and a good fit for spaces where running a dedicated line is impractical. The tradeoff is that you refill by hand, so keep the reservoir topped off during heavy use, and always drain the water fully before storing or transporting the machine.

Who should buy what

If you love soft, chewable pellets and drink a lot of sodas or iced coffee, a nugget ice maker is worth the extra cost and slower start time. Frequent hosts and anyone who wants ice on demand for water and mixed drinks are usually happiest with a fast bullet-ice model.

For tight kitchens, dorms, or an office break room, prioritize a compact footprint and easy refilling over maximum output. Tailgaters, RV owners, and patio entertainers should focus on portability and weight so the machine travels well. Buyers who dislike upkeep should give real weight to a self-cleaning cycle. And if you simply want reliable ice without a plumbing project, almost any reservoir-fed model will serve you well.

Bottom line

Start with the ice you actually want to chew or sip around, then match production speed, bin size, and footprint to your space and how you entertain. A nugget model rewards texture lovers, while a portable ice maker rewards speed and mobility. Because no plumbing is involved, you can set one up almost anywhere and enjoy fresh ice the same day.
