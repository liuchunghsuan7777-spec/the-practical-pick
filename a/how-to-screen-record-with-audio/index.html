How to Screen Record with Audio on Windows, Mac, iPhone & Android

Quick answer: Every modern device can record its screen with sound using a built-in tool — no app to install. The part people get wrong is the audio, so turn on the right source: Windows 11 (Win + Shift + R, then toggle the mic and system-audio icons), Mac (Shift + Cmd + 5 → Options → pick a Microphone), iPhone (Control Center → long-press Screen Recording → Microphone), and Android (Quick Settings → Screen record → choose Device audio, Microphone, or both).

Most "how to screen record" guides show you how to start a recording and stop there. But the thing that actually trips people up is sound — the clip comes out silent, or it captures your voice but not the app, or the app but not your voice. This guide covers the built-in recorder on each platform, leads with the audio step on every one, and ends with a single troubleshooting checklist for "why does my screen recording have no sound?"

Heads-up: operating systems rename menus and move buttons every year. The steps below are written for current OS versions as of late 2026. If a label looks slightly different on your device, look for the closest equivalent — the flow is the same.

How to screen record on Windows 11 (with audio)

Windows 11 records your screen through the Snipping Tool, which now does video as well as screenshots.

  • Press Windows + Shift + R to open the recording overlay (or open Snipping Tool and switch from Snip to Record).
  • Drag to select the area you want, then click Start. A short countdown begins the capture. (Source: Microsoft.)
  • When you're done, click Stop. Recordings save as MP4 in Videos > Screen Recordings. (Source: Microsoft.)

The audio part. As of Windows 11 version 24H2, the Snipping Tool can capture both your microphone and the system (app) audio. In the toolbar, use the microphone icon and the speaker / system-audio icon to turn each one on or off before or during recording. There's also a setting to include the microphone by default whenever a recording starts. (Source: Microsoft; NinjaOne.)


In the Snipping Tool toolbar, the microphone icon and the system-audio (speaker) icon each toggle a sound source on or off.

⚠️ Version caveat: system-audio capture needs the 24H2 Snipping Tool update. On older builds the tool may record the mic only, with no speaker icon. If you don't see it, update Snipping Tool from the Microsoft Store. As a fallback, the Xbox Game Bar (Windows + G) is the other built-in recorder, mainly aimed at games and single apps.

How to screen record on a Mac (with audio)

On a Mac, the recorder lives in the on-screen Screenshot toolbar.

  • Press Shift + Command + 5 to open the toolbar with screen-recording controls.
  • Choose to record the entire screen or a selected portion (recent macOS also adds "Record Selected Window"), then click Record.
  • To stop, click the Stop button in the menu bar. (Source: Apple Support.)

The audio catch — read this. Click Options in the toolbar and pick a Microphone so a checkmark appears. That records your voice only. The built-in macOS recorder does not capture the Mac's own internal/system audio by default — so app sound, music, or a video's audio won't be in your recording unless you add a separate route.

⚠️ This is the single most common Mac "no sound" complaint. To record internal/system audio on a Mac, you need a third-party virtual-audio driver or recorder — the built-in tool can't do it alone. (Apple's docs only mention Microphone capture; Apple updates these options yearly, so re-check on your macOS version.)

This Mac limitation is the most legitimate reason to reach for a third-party tool, which we compare honestly near the end.

How to screen record on iPhone or iPad (with sound)

iPhone and iPad record from Control Center.

  • Add the control first (one-time): open Control Center (swipe down from the top-right corner), touch and hold the background, tap Add a Control, and choose Screen Recording from the gallery.
  • Record: open Control Center and tap the Screen Recording button — a 3-second countdown starts the capture.
  • Stop: tap the red time indicator at the top of the screen, then Stop (or reopen Control Center and tap the button again). The video saves to Photos. (Source: Apple Support.)

The audio part. By default, iPhone records system/app sound but not your voice. To narrate, touch and hold the Screen Recording button before you start, then tap Microphone so it turns red. (Source: Apple Support.)


Long-press the Screen Recording button, then tap Microphone (it turns red) to add your voice to the capture.

How to screen record on Android (with audio)

Built-in screen recording arrived on Android 11, so most current phones have it with no app needed.

  • Swipe down from the top twice to open the full Quick Settings panel.
  • Tap Screen record (some phones say Screen Recorder). If the tile isn't there, tap the pencil / edit icon and drag the tile in.
  • A countdown starts the capture; pull down Quick Settings again and tap Stop to finish. The video lands in your Gallery / Photos. (Source: How-To Geek; Google Android Community.)

The audio part. Before recording starts, Android asks for the sound source — pick Device audio (app and media sound), Microphone (your voice), Device audio and microphone (both), or No audio. Choosing the wrong one here is the usual reason an Android recording comes out silent. (Source: How-To Geek; Digital Citizen.)

⚠️ Brand caveat: exact wording and placement differ between Pixel (stock Android), Samsung One UI, and other skins — some tuck "Record audio" or "Show touches" toggles in slightly different spots. Steps may vary slightly by phone brand and Android version.

Why does my screen recording have no sound? (the fix)

If your recording is silent or missing one side of the audio, run down this checklist — the cause is almost always one of these:

  • Wrong audio source selected. This is #1. Make sure the right toggle is on: system/device audio for app sound, microphone for your voice, or both. (Mac built-in = mic only; see below.)
  • The device is muted or volume is at zero. Some recorders capture system audio at the current output level — a muted device can record silence.
  • Bluetooth headphones are intercepting the audio. When sound is routed to Bluetooth earbuds or headphones, the recorder may miss it. Disconnect them and try again.
  • The app blocks recording. Some streaming and DRM-protected apps intentionally produce a black screen or silent recording to protect copyrighted content. That's by design, not a bug.
  • On a Mac, you're trying to capture system audio. The built-in tool only records the microphone — internal sound needs a third-party audio driver.
  • On Windows, the Snipping Tool is outdated. System-audio capture needs the 24H2 update; older builds record mic only. Update from the Microsoft Store.

The part most guides skip — System audio vs. microphone: "System" (or "device") audio is the sound your apps play — music, game audio, a video's soundtrack. "Microphone" is your own voice. A silent recording almost always means the wrong one was selected. Android lets you pick either or both; iPhone records system audio and needs the mic toggled on for your voice; the Mac built-in recorder captures the mic only.

When a third-party screen recorder is worth it

The whole point of this guide is that you usually don't need to download anything. But the built-in tools have real limits, and there are a few honest cases where a dedicated recorder earns its place. Here's a neutral comparison so you can decide — these are reference notes, not a sales pitch.

Best for most people — The free built-in recorder: Windows 11 (Snipping Tool), Mac (Shift+Cmd+5), iPhone/iPad (Control Center), and Android 11+ all record the screen with no download. Best for quick clips, demos, and tutorials with your voice. Limits: no editing or trimming, no webcam overlay, and the Mac version can't capture internal/system audio.

Mac internal sound — Recording system audio on a Mac: The built-in macOS recorder captures the microphone only — it won't grab the Mac's own app/music/video sound. To record internal/system audio you need a third-party virtual-audio driver or a recorder that bundles one. This is the most common legitimate reason a Mac user reaches for a paid tool. Check current pricing and macOS compatibility at the maker.

If you need to edit — Webcam overlay, trimming & captions: Built-in tools record but don't edit. If you want a webcam bubble, trimmed clips, callouts, zooms, or captions, a dedicated screen recorder/editor handles it in one place. Useful for course creators, support videos, and polished tutorials. Compare features and check current pricing at the maker — many offer a free tier or trial.

For heavy recordings — Long sessions, 4K & multi-source audio: For long tutorials, high-resolution captures, or mixing several audio sources (app sound plus mic plus a second input), a pro-grade recorder gives more control over quality, frame rate, and audio routing than the built-in options. Most everyday clips never need this — only step up if the free tools fall short. Pricing varies by plan; confirm at the maker.

FAQ

How do I record my screen with sound?
Start the built-in recorder for your device, then choose the audio source before (or during) the recording: system/device audio for app sound, microphone for your voice, or both where the device allows it. Picking the right source is the step most people miss.

Why is there no sound on my screen recording?
Usually the wrong audio source was selected, or the device was muted, the audio was routed to Bluetooth headphones, or the app blocks recording (some streaming/DRM apps record a silent or black screen on purpose). On a Mac, the built-in tool only captures the mic, not internal sound.

Can I record system/internal audio on a Mac?
Not with the built-in tool. Shift + Cmd + 5 captures the microphone only. To record the Mac's own sound (music, app audio, a video), you need a third-party virtual-audio driver or recorder.

Where are my screen recordings saved?
Windows: Videos > Screen Recordings. Mac: the desktop by default. iPhone/iPad: Photos. Android: the Gallery / Photos (often a "Movies" or "Screen recordings" folder).

Do I need an app to screen record?
No. Windows 11, Mac, iPhone/iPad, and Android 11+ all have screen recording built in. You only need a third-party app for extras like Mac system audio, a webcam overlay, editing, or long/4K captures.

The bottom line

  • No app needed: Windows 11, Mac, iPhone, and Android 11+ all record the screen out of the box.
  • The audio is the catch: turn on the right source — mic, system/device audio, or both. Android lets you pick all three; the Mac built-in tool is mic only.
  • Silent recording? Check the audio source first, then mute/volume, Bluetooth routing, and whether the app is blocking capture. On Windows, make sure Snipping Tool is on 24H2.
  • Third-party tools are only worth it for Mac system audio, a webcam overlay, editing, or long/4K recordings — and most clips never need them.

Pick your platform, flip the right audio switch, and your next recording will have the sound you expect.
