How to Mirror Your Phone to a TV (iPhone & Android, Wireless)

Mirror your iPhone or Android phone to a TV wirelessly with AirPlay or Google Cast, or go wired with an HDMI adapter — plus fixes for black screen and no sound.

Mirroring your phone to a TV lets you turn a small screen into a big one, whether you're sharing vacation photos, showing a video to the whole room, or playing a mobile game on the couch. Here's how to do it wirelessly on both iPhone and Android, plus a reliable wired backup for when Wi-Fi lets you down.

Mirror an iPhone Wirelessly with AirPlay

Apple's AirPlay is the built-in way to send your iPhone's screen to a compatible TV over Wi-Fi. It works best when your iPhone and TV are on the same Wi-Fi network.

To start:

  • Make sure your iPhone and TV (or streaming device) are connected to the same Wi-Fi network.
  • Open Control Center by swiping down from the top-right corner (or up from the bottom on older models).
  • Tap Screen Mirroring, then choose your TV from the list.
  • If a code appears on the TV, type it into your iPhone to confirm.

Many recent smart TVs support AirPlay natively. If yours doesn't, an Apple TV or a streaming stick that lists AirPlay support will add it. To stop, open Control Center again, tap Screen Mirroring, and choose Stop Mirroring.

Mirror an Android Phone (Google Cast and Miracast)

Android handles this in two main ways, and which one you use depends on your phone and TV.

  • Google Cast is built into Android and many TVs (especially Google TV and Chromecast-enabled sets). Open Quick Settings by swiping down from the top, tap Screen Cast (sometimes labeled Smart View, Cast, or Wireless Display), and pick your TV.
  • Miracast is a direct phone-to-TV standard that some Android phones and TVs support. Look for a "Wireless Display" or "Smart View" option in your settings.

Samsung phones use Smart View, which you'll find in Quick Settings. Naming varies a lot by brand, so if you don't see "Cast," check under Connected Devices or Display in your Settings app. As with iPhone, keep the phone and TV on the same Wi-Fi network for casting to work smoothly.

When Your TV Already Supports It vs. When You Need a Device

Newer smart TVs often include AirPlay, Google Cast, or both right out of the box. Check your TV's settings menu or its box for logos like "Works with Apple AirPlay" or "Chromecast built-in."

If your TV is older or doesn't list any casting support, you have a couple of options:

  • A streaming stick or device plugs into an HDMI port and adds casting, AirPlay, or both to almost any TV with an HDMI input. This is usually the simplest and most affordable upgrade for an older set.
  • A wireless HDMI transmitter kit is worth considering if you want a more reliable wireless link, especially from a laptop or a phone with an adapter. These kits send audio and video over their own dedicated connection instead of relying on your home Wi-Fi, which can help in crowded network environments.

The Wired Option: USB-C or Lightning to HDMI

When Wi-Fi is unreliable, or you just want zero lag, a physical cable is the most dependable route.

  • Newer Android phones and iPhones (iPhone 15 and later) use USB-C, so a USB-C-to-HDMI adapter connects the phone directly to your TV's HDMI port.
  • Older iPhones use Lightning, so you'll need a Lightning-to-HDMI adapter instead.

Plug the adapter into your phone, run an HDMI cable to the TV, and switch the TV to that HDMI input. A wired connection guarantees a steady picture and sound, which makes it a good choice for presentations, gaming, or long videos where dropouts would be frustrating. Note that not every phone supports video output over its port, so check your model's specs if the screen doesn't appear.

Troubleshooting Common Problems

  • Black screen or "no signal": Confirm the TV is set to the correct HDMI input, and that both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network for wireless setups. Restart the mirroring session.
  • No sound: Check that the TV isn't muted and that your phone's volume is up. Some apps mute mirrored audio for copyright reasons, so try a different app to test.
  • Laggy or choppy video: Move closer to your router, close background apps, or switch to a 5 GHz Wi-Fi network. For consistently smooth playback, a wired adapter or a wireless HDMI kit avoids network congestion entirely.
  • Can't find the TV: Make sure both devices are on the same network, that casting is enabled on the TV, and reboot both. Some networks with "client isolation" or guest Wi-Fi block device-to-device connections.

Bottom Line

Wireless mirroring is quick and cable-free when your phone and TV support the same standard. If your TV lacks casting, a streaming stick fills the gap; if you want rock-solid reliability, reach for a wired USB-C-to-HDMI adapter or a wireless HDMI kit. Match the method to your setup and you'll have your phone on the big screen in minutes.
