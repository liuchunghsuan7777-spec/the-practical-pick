Quick answer: Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage, then offload unused apps and turn on Optimize iPhone Storage for Photos. You can recover several gigabytes in a few minutes without deleting any photos. If the mysterious "System Data" is hogging space, you'll find the fix for that further down — it's the part most guides skip.

That "iPhone Storage Almost Full" banner shows up at the worst times — when you're trying to take a photo, install an update, or download an app. The good news is you almost never need to delete the things you actually care about. This guide leads with the fastest wins, then walks through nine ways to free up space, and finally demystifies the confusing "System Data" (a.k.a. "Other") category that won't seem to go away.

Heads-up: Apple changes menu labels between iOS versions (for example, older iOS used an "iCloud Photos" toggle while newer iOS says "Sync this iPhone"). The steps here are written for current iOS as of September 2026 — if a label looks slightly different on your phone, look for the closest equivalent.

First: see exactly what's eating your storage

Before you start deleting things at random, find out where the space actually went. It takes ten seconds and aims all your effort at the right target.

  • Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage.
  • At the top you'll see a color bar broken down by category — Apps, Photos, Media, Messages, System Data, and so on.
  • Below it, Apple shows personalized Recommendations (like "Review Large Attachments" or "Offload Unused Apps") and a per-app list sorted by size. (Source: Apple Support.)

Tap into the biggest categories first. For most people that's Photos and a couple of heavy apps — so that's where the quick wins are.

9 ways to free up space on iPhone (fastest first)

Work down this list in order. The first few clear the most space for the least effort.

1. Offload unused apps (keeps your data)

Offloading removes the app itself but keeps its documents and data. The app icon stays on your Home Screen with a small cloud symbol; tap it later and — if it's still on the App Store — it re-downloads with your data intact.

  • Turn it on automatically: Settings > Apps > App Store > Offload Unused Apps.
  • Or offload one app at a time from Settings > General > iPhone Storage, tap the app, then Offload App. (Source: Apple Support.)

2. Optimize Photos with iCloud (no photos deleted)

Photos and videos are usually the single biggest space hog. With iCloud Photos and Optimize iPhone Storage, full-resolution originals live in iCloud while smaller, space-saving copies stay on your device — so your library still looks complete.

  • Go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos and turn on Sync this iPhone.
  • Then select Optimize iPhone Storage. (Source: Apple Support.)

One honest caveat: this needs enough iCloud space. The free tier is 5 GB, so a large library may require a paid iCloud+ plan or another cloud backup (more on options below).

3. Clear Safari cache

Browsing builds up cached files over time. Clearing them is quick and safe:

  • Settings > Safari > Clear History and Website Data. (Source: Macworld.)

This also helps shrink "System Data" — more on that shortly.

4. Auto-delete old Messages and review large attachments

Old text threads and their photos, videos, and files quietly pile up. You can have your iPhone purge them on a schedule:

  • Settings > Messages > Keep Messages — change Forever to 1 Year or 30 Days to auto-delete old conversations and their attachments. (Source: Apple Support, Macworld.)
  • Then Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Review Large Attachments to delete big files one by one. (Source: Proton.)

5. Delete downloaded music, podcasts, and offline videos

Offline-saved content is invisible until you go looking for it. Downloaded songs, podcast back-catalogs, and saved streaming videos can total several gigabytes. Remove downloads you've already watched or can re-stream anytime.

6. Clear streaming app caches

Apps like music, video, and chat services cache gigabytes as you use them. Some let you clear the cache inside their own settings; for the rest, deleting and reinstalling the app wipes its cache (you'll sign back in afterward). This is the main lever for shrinking bloated System Data. (Source: Macworld, Avast.)

7. Remove duplicate and oversized videos

Video eats far more space than photos. In Photos, check the Duplicates album (Apple flags exact duplicates for you), and sort by size to find the giant 4K clips you no longer need.

8. Restart your iPhone

A plain restart clears temporary caches and can free up a bit of space on its own — and it sometimes makes a stuck System Data figure correct itself. It costs nothing, so it's worth a try before anything drastic.

9. Last resort: back up, then Erase All Content and Settings

If nothing else works and System Data is still wildly inflated, a clean reset rebuilds the device and purges the bloat:

  • Back up first (iCloud or computer) — this step erases everything.
  • Then Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Erase All Content and Settings, and restore from your backup. (Source: Macworld, AppleInsider.)

⚠️ Treat this as a genuine last resort. Confirm your backup is complete before you tap Erase — there's no undo.

What is "System Data" (a.k.a. "Other") and how to shrink it

This is the part that drives people up the wall: you delete apps and photos, yet a fat slice labeled System Data (older iOS called it "Other") won't budge.

What it actually is. System Data is a catch-all for non-removable assets and caches — Siri voices, fonts, dictionaries, system logs, the Spotlight index, Keychain, and local databases. You can't delete it directly; there's no "clear System Data" button. (Source: Macworld.)

How much is normal. Roughly 5–20 GB is typical. If yours sits consistently above ~20 GB, it has likely grown out of control and is worth tackling. (Source: Macworld, AppleInsider.)

The usual culprit. Streaming and chat apps that cache aggressively — music, video, and messaging services — are the most common cause of runaway System Data. (Source: Macworld, Avast.)

How to shrink it (in order):

  1. Clear Safari cache (Settings > Safari > Clear History and Website Data).
  2. Trim Messages history to 30 days or 1 year and clear large attachments.
  3. Offload or reinstall heavy streaming apps to dump their caches.
  4. Restart the iPhone so the figure recalculates.
  5. Only if it's still huge: back up and Erase All Content and Settings (step 9 above).

The part most guides skip — Shrink "System Data" / "Other"

System Data is non-removable caches (Siri voices, fonts, logs, Spotlight index) plus app caches — there's no direct delete button. Normal is about 5-20 GB; over ~20 GB means act. Shrink it: clear Safari cache, trim Messages history, reinstall cache-heavy streaming apps, then restart. Full purge of stubborn data may need a back-up-and-erase reset as a last resort.

Stop your iPhone from filling up again

Once you've cleared space, a few habits keep the warning from coming back:

  • Leave Offload Unused Apps on so rarely-used apps clear themselves.
  • Keep photos backed up to the cloud with Optimize iPhone Storage, so originals never have to live on the device.
  • Shoot at a lower resolution or frame rate if you don't need 4K/60 — Settings > Camera > Record Video lets you trade a little quality for a lot of space.
  • Clear Safari and Messages periodically instead of waiting for "Storage Almost Full."

If your photo library is too big — Cloud photo backup options

iCloud+ pairs tightest with iOS and Optimize iPhone Storage; Google Photos, OneDrive, and Dropbox also back up photos across devices. Free tiers are small (iCloud starts at 5 GB), so a large library usually needs a paid plan. Compare by included storage, cross-platform support, and price — check current plan pricing at each provider.

Honest caveat — Do "storage cleaner" apps help?

Mostly no. iOS sandboxes apps and manages its own caches, so a third-party cleaner can't reach most System Data, and many overstate their results. The built-in steps here — offload apps, optimize Photos, clear Safari and Messages — do the real work for free without an extra download.

If your phone is genuinely too small — When a bigger phone makes sense

If you constantly run out on a 64 GB or 128 GB model even after offloading and optimizing, a higher-capacity phone (or a well-priced refurbished/trade-in unit) may be the long-term fix. Weigh storage tiers against price, and try the free steps above first — most full-storage problems don't actually need new hardware. Check current prices at the seller.

FAQ

Why is my iPhone storage full when I deleted everything?
Two common reasons. First, deleted photos sit in Photos > Albums > Recently Deleted for 30 days and still take up space until you empty that album. Second, the space may be System Data (app caches), which deleting photos and apps won't touch — shrink it by clearing Safari, trimming Messages, and reinstalling cache-heavy apps.

Will offloading apps delete my data?
No. Offloading removes the app's program files but keeps your documents and data. Re-download the app and your data returns, as long as the app is still available on the App Store.

Does Optimize iPhone Storage delete my photos?
No. Your full-resolution originals stay safe in iCloud; only smaller, space-saving copies live on the device. Your photo library still appears complete, and full-res versions download when you need them.

How much System Data is normal on an iPhone?
Roughly 5–20 GB is typical. Consistently above about 20 GB suggests cached data has grown out of control — usually from streaming or messaging apps — and is worth clearing.

Can I clear System Data without resetting my iPhone?
Partly. You can shrink it by clearing Safari's cache, trimming Messages history, and reinstalling cache-heavy apps. There's no direct delete button, so a full purge of stubborn System Data sometimes needs a back-up-and-erase reset as a last resort.

Do storage cleaner apps actually work?
Be skeptical. Many third-party "cleaner" apps overstate what they can do — iOS already sandboxes apps and manages caches, so a cleaner can't reach most System Data anyway. The built-in steps above (offload, optimize Photos, clear caches) do the real work for free.

The bottom line

  • Fastest fix: Settings > General > iPhone Storage, then offload unused apps and turn on Optimize iPhone Storage for Photos — gigabytes back, zero photos lost.
  • For the dreaded "System Data": clear Safari, trim Messages, reinstall cache-heavy streaming apps, and restart. Normal is ~5–20 GB; over ~20 GB means it's time to act.
  • "Deleted everything and still full"? Empty Photos > Recently Deleted, then attack System Data.
