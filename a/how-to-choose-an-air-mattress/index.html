How to Choose an Air Mattress (Guests & Camping)

Pick the right air mattress by height, built-in vs external pump, and durability — for overnight guests or camping trips.

Whether you're hosting weekend visitors or heading into the backcountry, the right inflatable bed makes the difference between a restful night and a restless one. This guide walks through the features that matter most so you can match an air mattress to how and where you actually plan to use it.

What to look for

A few core features separate a mattress you'll love from one that ends up in the closet. Start here before comparing anything else.

Height and thickness. Taller, double-height mattresses sit higher off the floor, which makes getting in and out easier and adds a more traditional bed feel. Lower-profile models pack down smaller and weigh less, so they travel better. Think about who's sleeping on it: older guests or anyone with limited mobility often appreciate the extra height, while a slim design is friendlier for carrying and storage.

Built-in versus external pump. An air mattress with a built-in pump is the most convenient option for home use because inflation and deflation happen at the push of a button with no extra gear to lose. External pumps, by contrast, keep the mattress itself lighter and let you swap in a different pump if one fails. For trips away from an outlet, a rechargeable air pump is worth considering since it inflates the bed without needing to be plugged in.

Material durability and puncture resistance. Look for thicker, multi-layer construction and reinforced seams, which tend to hold up better to repeated use. Flocked or textured tops help keep sheets and sleepers from sliding around. No material is puncture-proof, so it also helps if the mattress comes with a patch kit or if replacement patches are easy to find.

Size. Air mattresses come in familiar dimensions from twin up through king. Match the size to your space and your sleepers, and remember that a larger mattress takes longer to inflate and needs more room to store. Measure the spot where you'll set it up, especially in a smaller guest room or tent.

Weight capacity. Every mattress lists a maximum supported weight. Choose one rated comfortably above the combined weight of whoever will use it, including couples sharing a larger size. Staying within the rated capacity helps the seams and internal structure last longer.

Guest use versus camping

The best choice depends heavily on setting, because a mattress built for a spare bedroom and one built for a trail have different priorities.

For guests at home, comfort and convenience lead. A taller mattress near an outlet, ideally an air mattress with a built-in pump, sets up in minutes and feels closest to a standard bed. You can prioritize plushness and height because you don't have to carry it anywhere, and storage is usually just a matter of tucking it into a closet between visits.

For camping, portability and packed size take over. A bulky home mattress is awkward to haul and hard to keep warm against cold ground. Many campers prefer a self-inflating camping sleeping pad, which unrolls, opens its valve, and expands on its own with minimal effort. Pads like these pack down small, add a layer of insulation between you and the ground, and shrug off rough surfaces better than a tall household mattress. If you do bring a traditional air mattress on a car-camping trip, pair it with a rechargeable air pump so you're not dependent on a power source at the site.

Comfort and setup considerations

Firmness is largely about air volume, so being able to top off the mattress matters. Air naturally settles overnight and cool temperatures can make a bed feel softer by morning, which is why a quick reinflation option is handy. Give any new mattress a full inflation and a short rest before its first real use so the material can stretch and settle.

Setup time is easy to overlook. A built-in or rechargeable pump can have a bed ready in a couple of minutes, while manual inflation asks for more patience. Also plan for deflation and packing, since a mattress that folds and rolls neatly is far easier to store or carry than one that resists going back in its bag.

Care and longevity

A little maintenance goes a long way. Keep the mattress on a clean, smooth surface away from sharp objects, and consider a mat or blanket underneath outdoors. Wipe it down and make sure it's fully dry before storing to avoid mildew, and store it loosely rather than crammed tight. Close valves fully to prevent slow leaks, and keep a patch kit on hand so a small puncture doesn't end the mattress's life.

Bottom line

Choose based on where you'll sleep. For guests, height, comfort, and an air mattress with a built-in pump win out. For camping, favor low weight and small packed size, leaning toward a self-inflating camping sleeping pad or a standard mattress paired with a rechargeable air pump. Match size and weight capacity to your sleepers, prioritize durable material, and you'll have a bed that's ready whenever you need it.
