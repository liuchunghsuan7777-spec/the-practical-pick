How to Clean a Dishwasher with Vinegar and Baking Soda

Quick answer: To clean a dishwasher, twist out and rinse the bottom filter, then run an empty hot cycle with a bowl of white vinegar on the top rack, follow with a short hot cycle using 1 cup of baking soda sprinkled across the bottom, and wipe the door gasket. Run the vinegar and baking soda as separate cycles, never at the same time, and repeat the whole routine about once a month.

If your dishwasher smells musty or sour, or your dishes come out gritty, filmy, or still dirty, the machine itself is almost always the problem — not your detergent. The good news is you can usually fix it today with white vinegar and baking soda you already have. Below you'll find the exact steps for the filter, spray arms, and odor, a how-often table, a quick troubleshooting guide, and the one smell that means it's time to check your drain, not keep scrubbing.

Why does my dishwasher smell (or stop cleaning)?

A dishwasher is warm, damp, and full of food residue — a perfect home for odor-causing bacteria. Most problems trace back to three spots:

  • Trapped food in the filter. The bottom filter catches food scraps, grease, and oil. Left alone, that gunk grows bacteria and turns the sour, musty, or rotten-egg smell you notice when you open the door. A clogged filter is the single most common cause of both odor and poor cleaning.
  • Clogged spray arms. The spinning arms shoot water up onto your dishes through small jet holes. Hard-water mineral deposits and food debris block those holes, so the water can't reach everything — and dishes come out filmy or gritty.
  • Residue on the gasket and door edges. The rubber door seal and the lip around the door trap moisture and soap scum, a hidden spot where mildew quietly grows.

When the smell is your drain, not your dishwasher

Here's the part most guides skip. If you've cleaned the filter and deodorized the machine but a persistent rotten-egg or sewage smell survives, the odor may be coming from the drain hose or your plumbing, not the dishwasher itself. At that point, more scrubbing won't help — it's worth checking the drain connection or calling a plumber rather than re-cleaning the drum a fifth time.

Filter Smell vs. Drain Smell
Not every dishwasher odor comes from the machine itself. A musty or sour smell is usually food and grease trapped in the bottom filter, which cleaning fixes. But a persistent rotten-egg or sewage smell that survives a full clean more often points to a drain hose or plumbing issue, not the dishwasher. If the smell stays after you have cleaned the filter and deodorized the machine, check the drain connection or call a plumber rather than re-scrubbing. What you'll need

A short checklist before you start. You only need basic supplies, and you almost certainly have most of them already.

  • White vinegar (about 1–2 cups)
  • Baking soda (about 1 cup)
  • A soft toothbrush or small detail brush
  • A microfiber cloth
  • A dishwasher-safe bowl or measuring cup
  • A toothpick or thin wire (for the spray-arm jets)
  • Optional: a dishwasher cleaner tablet and a rinse aid for upkeep
Info
Cleaning Agent: Vinegar, Baking Soda, or Tablets
You have a few options for the actual cleaning agent. White vinegar is a budget, low-residue choice that cuts grease and most musty odors and dissolves hard-water film; used too often, though, mild acid can wear rubber seals over time. Baking soda is a gentle deodorizer and brightener best run as its own short cycle. Dedicated dishwasher cleaner tablets are the pre-measured, mess-free option that pairs well with a machine's clean cycle, trading a little cost for convenience. Match your choice to how strong the smell is and what your manual allows, and never run vinegar and baking soda in the same cycle. Safety first: a couple of important rules

These matter, so it's worth saying plainly:

  • Run vinegar and baking soda in separate cycles — never together. When they meet they fizz and neutralize each other, canceling out the cleaning power. Vinegar first, baking soda second, two separate cycles.
  • Don't use bleach in a stainless-steel dishwasher (or one with stainless parts). Bleach corrodes and damages stainless steel. And as a general rule, never mix bleach with vinegar — the combination releases toxic fumes.
  • Skip steel wool and abrasive pads. They leave micro-scratches on stainless surfaces. A soft brush and microfiber cloth are all you need.
  • Check your appliance manual for where your specific filter is and which cleaners the manufacturer approves.

Step 1: Clean the filter (do this first)

The filter is the biggest single fix for both odor and poor cleaning, so always start here.

  1. Empty the dishwasher and pull out the bottom rack so you can reach the floor of the tub.
  2. Twist out the filter. Following your manual, locate the cylindrical filter on the tub floor and turn it (usually counterclockwise) to lift it out.
  3. Rinse and scrub it. Hold the filter under warm running water to flush off loose food. Then either sprinkle baking soda inside and scrub gently with a soft toothbrush, or soak it about 20 minutes in equal parts warm water and white vinegar. Rinse and let it air-dry.
  4. Reinstall the filter and confirm it locks firmly back in place. A loose filter can rattle and let debris back through.

Step 2: Clear the spray arms

If your dishes come out gritty or covered in a white film, the spray-arm jets are likely clogged with hard-water deposits.

  1. Remove the spray arms if your model allows (many lift or unscrew off — check your manual). Soak them in white vinegar to loosen food debris and mineral buildup.
  2. Clear each jet hole by poking it with a toothpick or thin wire, then rinse the arms under warm water.
  3. Reinstall and test. Put the arms back and spin them by hand to confirm they turn freely with nothing blocking them. Arms that don't spin can't rinse your dishes.

Step 3: Deodorize with vinegar, then baking soda

Now the part that kills the smell. This is two separate hot cycles — vinegar first, baking soda second.

  1. Vinegar cycle. With the dishwasher empty, place a dishwasher-safe bowl with 1–2 cups of white vinegar upright on the top rack. Run a hot-water cycle with no detergent and the heat-dry setting turned off. The vinegar washes through the machine and cuts grease and odor.
  2. Baking soda cycle. Remove the bowl, then sprinkle about 1 cup of baking soda across the bottom of the tub. Run a short, hot cycle (heat-dry off). The baking soda freshens and brightens the interior.

Keep them as two passes. Combined, vinegar and baking soda just fizz themselves out and leave you with a clean-looking but unscrubbed machine.

Step 4: Wipe the gasket, door, and exterior

The deep clean is done — finish the spots the cycles can't reach.

  • Door gasket and edges. Wipe the rubber seal and the lip around the door with a damp microfiber cloth. This is a classic hidden mildew spot, and a 30-second wipe keeps it from coming back.
  • Detergent dispenser. Wipe out any caked-on detergent or rinse-aid residue.
  • Exterior. For a stainless-steel front, use glass cleaner or a dedicated stainless-steel cleaner and wipe with the grain — never abrasive pads or steel wool.

How often should you clean a dishwasher?

A quick deep clean once a month keeps odor and film from ever building up. Different parts need different attention:

Part How often What to do
Filter Monthly (3–6× a year if you scrape plates and run it less often) Twist out, rinse, scrub with baking soda or soak in vinegar
Full deodorize (vinegar + baking soda) Monthly Two separate hot cycles
Spray arms Every few months, or when dishes turn filmy Soak in vinegar, clear the jet holes
Gasket / door edges Weekly wipe Damp microfiber cloth

Never let a dishwasher go longer than about six months without a clean. Run the routine more often if you rarely scrape plates or live with hard water.

Why is my dishwasher still not cleaning dishes?

If dishes come out dirty or filmy even after a clean, run through this quick checklist:

Symptom Likely cause Fix
Food still on dishes Clogged filter or spray arms Clean the filter (Step 1), clear the arms (Step 2)
White film / spots Hard-water minerals Use a rinse aid; soak arms and filter in vinegar
Dishes not getting hot/clean Water not hot enough Run the hot tap at the sink before starting the cycle
Some dishes missed Overloading / blocked arms Don't overpack; make sure nothing blocks the arms from spinning
Cloudy glasses Too much or wrong detergent Adjust detergent amount; add rinse aid

If the filter, arms, water temperature, and loading all check out and dishes still come out dirty, the issue may be a worn pump or heating element — that's a repair call rather than a cleaning one.

FAQ

Why does my dishwasher smell like rotten eggs?
Usually trapped food rotting in the filter and drain area — clean the filter (Step 1) and deodorize (Step 3) first. If a rotten-egg or sewage smell survives a full clean, it more likely points to a drain or plumbing issue, so check the drain connection or call a plumber.

Can I use vinegar and baking soda together?
No. Run them in separate cycles — vinegar first, baking soda second. Together they fizz and neutralize each other, which cancels the cleaning effect.

Can I put vinegar in the dishwasher's detergent cup?
It's better to put the vinegar in a dishwasher-safe bowl on the top rack so it releases gradually through the cycle rather than dumping all at once. Use vinegar cleans about monthly to be gentle on rubber seals.

Why are my dishes coming out with a white film?
That's almost always hard-water mineral buildup. Soak the spray arms and filter in vinegar, clear the jet holes, and add a rinse aid to keep the film from returning.

How often should I clean the dishwasher filter?
About once a month for most homes. If you scrape plates well and run the machine less often, every couple of months (3–6 times a year) is enough — just don't go longer than six months.

Are dishwasher cleaner tablets worth it?
They're a convenient, pre-measured option, especially with a machine's dedicated clean cycle. Vinegar and baking soda work just as well for most odors and film; tablets simply trade a little cost for convenience.

Bottom line

A smelly dishwasher or one that won't clean almost always comes down to a clogged filter, blocked spray arms, and built-up residue — and the fix is simple. Rinse the filter, clear the arms, run a vinegar cycle followed by a separate baking-soda cycle, and wipe the gasket. Make it a monthly habit, and remember the one exception: a stubborn rotten-egg or sewage smell that survives a clean usually means it's time to look at the drain, not the drum.
