How to Choose an E-Reader (Screen, Light & Waterproofing)

E-ink screen, warm light, storage, and waterproofing — pick an e-reader that's easy on the eyes for long reading.

Buying your first dedicated e-reader, or upgrading an older one, comes down to a handful of choices about the screen, the lighting, and how much water resistance you actually need. This guide walks through what matters so you can match a device to how and where you like to read.

Why choose an e-reader over a tablet

It's tempting to think a tablet or phone can do the same job, and technically it can. But the reading experience is very different. A dedicated e-reader uses an e-ink display that mimics the look of printed paper. It reflects ambient light instead of shining it directly at you, so it stays readable in bright sunlight where a glossy tablet screen turns into a mirror.

Tablets are backlit color screens built for video, games, browsing, and apps. That makes them versatile but also distracting, and their batteries are measured in hours. An e-reader does one thing and does it well: it displays text. Because e-ink only draws power when the page changes, a single charge can last for weeks of regular reading. If you want a focused, book-like experience, a standard e-reader is the better tool. If you want an all-purpose device, a tablet makes more sense.

What to look for

Screen size and resolution. Most e-readers land in the 6-inch to 7-inch range, which is comfortable to hold one-handed and slips into a bag or large pocket. Larger 8-inch and up models give you more text per page and suit PDFs, comics, or anyone who prefers bigger type, but they're heavier. Resolution is usually described in pixels per inch (ppi); a higher ppi means crisper text with smooth letter edges. Look for a sharp, high-density display so small fonts stay clean.

Front light and warm light. Early e-readers had no lighting at all, so you needed a lamp. Modern models include a built-in front light that shines across the surface of the screen rather than into your eyes, letting you read in a dark room. The next step up is adjustable color temperature. An e-reader with warm light lets you shift the screen from a cool white toward a warmer amber tone, which many people prefer for evening reading. Being able to dial both brightness and warmth is one of the nicest upgrades you can get.

Waterproofing. If you read in the bath, by the pool, or at the beach, water resistance is worth prioritizing. A waterproof e-reader is rated to survive being splashed or briefly submerged, so a dropped device or a spilled drink isn't a disaster. Water resistance is typically expressed as an IPX rating; a higher number means more protection. Not everyone needs it, but if your reading happens near water, it's a feature you'll be glad you paid for.

Storage. Storage determines how many titles you can keep on the device at once. Plain text-based books are small, so even a modest amount of storage holds thousands of them. If you read a lot of image-heavy content like comics, graphic novels, or illustrated PDFs, more storage helps because those files are larger. For most readers, the base storage is plenty.

Battery life. Thanks to e-ink, battery life is measured in weeks rather than hours. Real-world life depends on how bright you keep the light and how often you turn pages, but you can generally expect to charge only occasionally. Check the charging port type as well, since newer models tend to use a more common connector.

Ecosystem and formats. This is easy to overlook but important. Each e-reader is tied to a bookstore and works best with that store's library, and some are more open to outside file formats than others. Think about where you already buy or borrow books, whether you use your local library's lending app, and which file types you own. Picking a device that plays nicely with your existing collection saves a lot of friction later.

Matching the device to your habits

Start with where you read. A commuter who reads on the train is well served by a compact standard e-reader with a good front light. A night-owl reader who winds down before bed will appreciate an e-reader with warm light. Someone who reads poolside or in the tub should put a waterproof e-reader at the top of the list. Once you know your setting, the size, lighting, and durability choices tend to sort themselves out.

Bottom line

An e-reader is a focused, long-lasting companion for people who love to read. Decide on screen size and sharpness first, then choose the lighting and water resistance that fit where you read, and confirm the device works with the books and library you already use. Get those basics right and you'll have a reader that feels effortless for years.
