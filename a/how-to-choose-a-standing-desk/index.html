How to Choose a Standing Desk (Full Desk vs Converter)

Full electric standing desk or a desktop converter? Compare stability, height range, motors, presets, and add-ons to pick the right sit-stand setup.

Working from home means spending hours at your desk, and more people are looking for ways to move a little more during the day. A standing desk is one of the simplest upgrades you can make, but the choice between a full electric desk and a desktop converter trips up a lot of shoppers. Here is how to decide.

Full electric standing desk: the all-in-one option

An electric standing desk replaces your existing desk entirely. Motors in the legs raise and lower the whole surface, so everything on top, your monitor, keyboard, coffee, and clutter, moves together at the push of a button.

The upside is a clean, stable setup with a wide height range and plenty of surface area. Most electric models glide smoothly and let you save preset heights so switching from sitting to standing takes a second. The trade-offs are cost and commitment. A full desk is the pricier route, it takes up real floor space, and assembly can be a two-person job. If you have room, a dedicated home office, and you want one purpose-built piece of furniture, this is usually the better long-term choice.

Standing-desk converter: upgrade what you already own

A standing-desk converter sits on top of your current desk and lifts your monitor and keyboard when you want to stand. Nothing else about your setup changes. You keep your existing desk, drawers, and layout, and simply raise the converter when you feel like standing.

Converters are cheaper, need no assembly of a full frame, and are easy to move or remove. They are a great fit for renters, small apartments, shared spaces, or anyone who wants to test standing before investing in a full desk. The downsides: they occupy usable desktop space even when lowered, the height range is narrower than a full desk, and heavier units can feel bulky. If you are happy with your current desk and just want the option to stand, a converter is the low-commitment answer.

What to look for

Whichever route you pick, a few specs separate a desk you will actually use from one that frustrates you.

  • Height range. Make sure the low and high ends fit your body. Taller and shorter users should check the numbers rather than assuming; a range that works for someone 5'8" may leave you hunched or reaching.
  • Motor and lifting capacity. For a full desk, confirm the capacity comfortably exceeds the combined weight of your monitors, arms, and accessories. A single-motor desk is fine for lighter loads and lower budgets, while a dual-motor desk generally lifts more, moves faster, and stays steadier under heavy setups.
  • Stability and wobble. Wobble at standing height is the most common complaint. Look for a sturdy frame and read what others say about shake when typing tall.
  • Desktop size. Measure your space and your gear. Dual monitors need more width and depth than a single laptop setup.
  • Programmable presets. Saved height buttons make it far more likely you will actually alternate positions instead of leaving the desk parked all day.
  • Cable management. Trays, clips, or channels keep cords from snagging as the desk moves, which matters more than it sounds.

Setting your height and alternating positions

Getting the height right is what makes standing feel good. As a general starting point, set the surface so your elbows rest at roughly a 90-degree angle with forearms about parallel to the floor, and position your screen so the top of the monitor is around eye level. Keep your wrists neutral rather than bent up or down.

You do not need to stand all day, and standing still for long stretches is not the goal either. Many people find it comfortable to alternate, standing for a stretch, then sitting, and shifting whenever they feel like it. Presets make these swaps effortless. Listen to your body and change positions before you feel stiff. These are general comfort tips, not medical advice; if you have specific concerns, check with a professional.

Useful add-ons

A few extras go a long way. An anti-fatigue mat is the most popular companion for a standing setup, adding a cushioned surface that makes standing stretches more comfortable underfoot. A monitor arm frees up desktop space and helps you dial in screen height independently of the desk. A keyboard tray, cable organizer, and a small under-desk footrest round out a setup you will want to use.

Bottom line

If you want a permanent, spacious, rock-steady workstation and have the room and budget, go with a full electric standing desk. If you want flexibility, lower cost, or a low-risk way to try standing, a converter on your existing desk is the smart pick. Either way, prioritize a comfortable height range, real stability, and easy presets, add an anti-fatigue mat, and you will have a setup that actually gets you moving.
